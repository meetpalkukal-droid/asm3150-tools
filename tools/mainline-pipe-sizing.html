About this calculator

This is Example 8.5's problem, generalized: given a flow rate and a target velocity, pick the smallest standard pipe (Table 8.5) that keeps you under it, then carry that pipe's friction loss (Hazen-Williams, Eq. 8.11) and the site's elevation change through to a predicted outlet pressure (Eq. 8.10).

For a detailed fittings tally instead of the single lumped "additional minor losses" field here, use the Pipeline Energy, Friction & Minor Losses tool, which shares the same Hazen-Williams and pressure-change logic.

Worked example this reproduces: Example 8.5 — 700 gpm through a PVC PIP mainline, 600 ft long, falling 25 ft, 35 psi inlet. The tool recommends the 8-in PIP size (inside diameter 7.658 in, keeping velocity under 5 ft/s), giving a friction loss of about 0.39 psi/100 ft and an outlet pressure of about 43.5 psi. These are the tool's defaults.
