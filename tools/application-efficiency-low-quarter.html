About this calculator

Deep percolation happens for two different reasons: the field isn't watered perfectly uniformly (nonuniformity), and/or more water is applied on average than the root zone can hold (excessive application). The low-quarter depth (dLQ) — the average depth received by the driest 25% of the field — is used as the practical "management depth": irrigating to dLQ results in about 90% of the field being adequately watered.

dev = dg − dz − dr   —   dLQ = DU × dz   —   de = min(dLQ, SWD)   —   ELQ = 100% × de / da

If dLQ < SWD, the field isn't even fully refilled at the low quarter — the effective depth is limited by nonuniformity, not over-application. If dLQ > SWD, the low quarter already exceeds what the root zone can hold — the effective depth is capped by SWD, and the excess is deep percolation from over-application. Either way, ELQ tells you what fraction of the water pulled from the source is doing useful work for the crop.

Textbook Example 5.2: dz=2.0 in, DU=0.75, da=2.2 in, no runoff, SWD=1.6 in → dLQ=1.5 in (< SWD), de=1.5 in, ELQ=68%.
Example 5.3 repeats this with SWD=1.2 in instead → now dLQ > SWD, so de=1.2 in and ELQ=55%.
