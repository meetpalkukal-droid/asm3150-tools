About this calculator

As water infiltrates, each soil layer fills to field capacity before the wetting front advances into the next layer. The depth of water a layer can still absorb is its water deficit per unit depth (θfc − θv) times its thickness — the same soil water deficit (SWD) calculation as the scheduling calculator, applied layer by layer:

SWDlayer = (θfc − θv) × thickness

The applied water fills each layer's deficit in turn; wherever it runs out mid-layer, that's the wetting front. If the front goes deeper than the root zone, the water that filled soil below the roots is lost to deep percolation — it can't be used by the crop, and it's how nitrate and other mobile chemicals leach out of the root zone.

This calculator carries full precision through every step by default. The textbook's worked example rounds each layer's SWD to 1 decimal place before moving to the next step, which compounds noticeably here (a partial-layer depth divided by a small deficit amplifies small rounding differences). Full precision gives a wetting front of about 47.7 in and deep percolation of about 0.70 in for Example 2.4/2.5, versus the textbook's rounded 46.7 in and 0.6 in — check "Match textbook rounding" above to reproduce the book's numbers exactly.

Textbook Examples 2.4 & 2.5: 4 in of water infiltrates a profile of 0–12 in (θfc=0.34, θv=0.20), 12–30 in (θfc=0.40, θv=0.33), and 30+ in (θfc=0.30, θv=0.24, unbounded). With a 36-in root zone, deep percolation ≈ 0.6–0.7 in. Click "Load textbook Example 2.4 & 2.5" above to see it worked through.
