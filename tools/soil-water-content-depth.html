About this calculator

Mass water content (θm) is the easiest to measure directly (weigh, dry, reweigh) but isn't directly useful for irrigation decisions. Converting it to volumetric water content (θv) using bulk density, then to an equivalent depth of water over a soil layer, makes it directly comparable to an irrigation or rainfall depth — which is what Step 4 uses to figure out how much more water (if any) is needed to reach a target water content.

Textbook Example 2.2: An 80 cm³ soil sample weighs 120 g wet and 100 g dry. θm = 0.20, θv = 0.25, current depth in the top 12 in (1 ft) = 3.0 in. Raising θv to 0.30 requires 0.6 in more water. Click "Load textbook Example 2.2" above to see it worked through.
